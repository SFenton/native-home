# Journey speed rollout

Owner, 2026-11-02: make the XCUITest journeys faster one class at a time, without weakening what they check. The levers are app reuse (one launch for the tests of a class instead of one per test), cheaper lookups (one `snapshot()` read instead of a request per property, the audit's `ElementFacts` and the single-walk `textUnderFloatingBar`), and fewer fixed sleeps (the configurable animation speed, `speed 30` where measured safe). Audit verdicts must stay identical.

## Totals (2026-11-04, rollout complete)

Every UI-test class has a row below. The 131 classes are grouped into 58 rows.

| | Test time |
|---|---|
| 40 rows with a measured baseline (iPhone, median of 3 runs) | 9,365 s (156 min) |
| The same rows after | 6,662 s (111 min) |
| **Saved** | **2,703 s (45 min), 29 %, 1.41×** |

How the totals were computed:
- "No saving" rows count as unchanged.
- Rows with nothing to time are left out. Those classes skip on the simulator, run only Live or on a device, or need a token.
- The shared `AppReuse` row and the Feedback follow-up are left out because their savings are already in their classes' rows.
- Medians from runs taken under high host load are soft, and the per-row notes say which.

The biggest wins came from app reuse plus one-snapshot reads:
- HomePages 2.85×
- ChipAlignment 3.2×
- AdaptiveLayout 2.5×
- LiveActivity 2.28×
- LightSheetCapture 2.2×
- Weather 2.1×
- ChoresSubPages 2.0×
- MediaRemote 1.9×
- HouseholdSettings 1.87×

These rows recorded no saving and no code change:
- LightAppearance, Chores (beyond the pilot), Login and SystemSettings, whose time is audits and launches.
- Capture, Live and Perf classes.

Left open, in the rows and in `build/handoff/test-speed-opportunities.md`:
- The AX5 audits are the largest remaining cost: 75 to 90 s for one audit that ends the app.
- Slow reveal drags.
- The 4 Chores typing tests launch fresh.
- The ResumeCycles 35 s grace could be shortened with `FENTON_BACKGROUND_GRACE_SECONDS`.
- Pre-existing failures: two LightAppearance system-appearance tests, AppIcon, Navigation.
- The owner's pick on the Chores due-line contrast labels.

What to try next, with evidence, steps and sources: [test-speed-roadmap](test-speed-roadmap.md).

## Method

- iPhone only, on the pinned simulator, `tools/ios_uitest.sh --only iphone --force --tests 'Class*'`, inside the usual devcap hold.
- Three runs a side (baseline commit, then after); report the median of the summed test time (`Test Case ... passed (N seconds)` lines) and of the wall time. Note the machine load average, since a loaded Mac inflates both sides.
- Launch counts come from the run log (app launches per class).
- A row is only added when the after-run passes and its audit verdicts match the baseline's.

## Host swift test (`@MainActor` suites) (2026-11-04)

Opportunity O4 of `build/handoff/test-speed-opportunities.md` assumed a full host `swift test` of about 30 minutes, with the `@MainActor` suites as its critical path. Measured on `claude/uikit-port` at 2431c9c4 (detached baseline worktree, `swift build --build-tests --enable-code-coverage` then `swift test --skip-build --enable-code-coverage`, each in a devcap `build`+`swift-test` hold; load average 140-230 throughout):

| Phase | Wall time |
|---|---|
| Compile (`--build-tests`, warm module cache) | 168 s |
| Run, 1st | 21 s (5 test runs: 333, 27, 433, 1804 and 81 tests = 2,678 tests, 348 suites; 2.9 + 1.3 + 1.8 + 8.2 + 0.02 s) |
| Run, 2nd (noise) | 20 s (2.7 + 1.3 + 1.9 + 7.9 + 0.02 s) |

Both runs pass with 0 skipped. The 30 minute figure is stale: it came from the SwiftUI era, when about ten minutes were hosted-window suites. The UIKit port removed those from the host run, and the longest single test now takes about 1.5 s (a deliberate sleep).

**Decision: no suite was moved.** The whole run is 20 s and the largest `@MainActor` candidate would save a fraction of the 8 s bundle at best, below the 1 s run-to-run noise. Against that stands the risk the order-independence rules name (suites that relied on serial order racing), plus two full reruns and a coverage comparison. Moved: none. Kept: all, including the survey's likeliest movers, which sit at 1-5 s total.

Survey (static, for whoever revisits): 113 of 228 test files mention `@MainActor`; about 49 of them reference no UI, run-loop or `EntityStore` name. Most of those still test `@MainActor` production models (`ScanItemModel`, `InventoryEditorModel`, `InventoryListModel`, `LocationReporter`, `WeatherForecastStore`, `TodoListController`, `GarageDoorController`, `OpenOnce`), so they cannot leave the main actor without a production change. Genuine candidates (nonisolated types): `BackdropBlurTests`, `PresenceLinkerTests`, `SensorReporterTests`, `ReconnectingPolicyTests`, `TelemetryTests`, `ResourceCensusTests`, `SignallingSocketTests`, and the HassDesign value-type suites that only `import SwiftUI` for `Color` or `CGSize` math (`DynamicGridSpanTests`, `TileLayoutTests`, `ColourContrastTests`, `DesignTokensAndFieldsTests`).
