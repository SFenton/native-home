# Header edge fade

> **What:** how scrolling content meets a page or modal header. Pages (since 2026-10-28) use the system's own scroll-edge effect and bar material; the fade below is for modals. **Read when:** touching a navigation bar background, a scroll-edge effect, a mask or a pinned header.

Owner, 2026-10-13: "Headers should not have the translucent background behind them in pages or modals. Content should fade as it scrolls up to the headers. Check FestivalNativeApps for a good implementation." Ported from FestivalNativeApps `ModalTopEdgeFade` (`apple/Sources/FestivalUI/Design/ModalTopEdgeFade.swift`, its `scroll-edge` pattern).

## Rules

- **R1. One mechanism.** `HassDesign/HeaderEdgeFade.swift` `headerEdgeFade()` is the only place that hides the navigation bar background (`toolbarBackground(.hidden, for: .navigationBar)`) or the iOS 26 top scroll-edge effect (`scrollEdgeEffectHidden`), and the only top-edge content mask. Pages get it from `pageChrome()`; modals from `HassModal` or `modalChrome()`. Feature folders never add their own.
- **R2. Clear at the header, opaque 40 pt below it.** Content is fully transparent at the header's bottom edge and fully drawn `HeaderEdgeFade.rampHeight` (40 pt, the web `useScrollMask` `DEFAULT_SIZE`) below it. Nothing scrolls visibly behind the title or toolbar items.
- **R3. At rest, nothing is dimmed.** The ramp grows with the first 40 pt of scrolling (`progress(scrollOffset:)`), read from `onScrollGeometryChange` (iOS 18+).
- **R4. Hard edge for accessibility.** Reduce Transparency or Increase Contrast make the ramp 0: content is cut cleanly at the header, never dimmed and never shown under it. iOS 17 has no scroll reading and also gets the hard edge.
- **R5. Apply it inside the stack, on the page.** The modifier measures the page's top safe-area inset (the bar, a large title included, and anything pinned with `safeAreaInset(edge: .top)` outside it, such as `modalTabBar`). Every page pushed inside a modal applies `modalChrome()` itself; every page pushed on a section stack applies `pageChrome()`.
- **R6. Backgrounds are never masked.** The page backdrop is the navigation container's background (`pageBackdrop()`), outside the masked content.
- **R7. Every page title is the system's inline title in the bar, always.** Three owner decisions. (1) 2026-10-27, verbatim: "Bring back the fade, but keep title centered instead of left aligned now". This **reverses** the owner's 2026-10-24 rule ("titles should be left-aligned when they enter the header/navbar space after scrolling") for the collapsing large title: on iPhone and on iPad with the flyout (`BarTitleStyle.collapsing`) the bar shows the system's centred inline title once the large title has collapsed. Reason: the custom `CollapsedTitleController` swapped a hosted SwiftUI `titleView` into the bar mid-collapse and measured 23 to 27 ms main-thread turns each time a scroll crossed the top (simulator, Rounds 5 and 6 in [performance](../testing/apple/performance.md)); the owner felt no loss with the system title. (2) 2026-10-27, verbatim: "Duo and compact iPad should follow system design patterns." The `.inBar` style (iPhone Duo, compact iPad: title always in the bar at one size, `.toolbarTitleDisplayMode(.inline)`) also drops the custom title: it is the system's inline title, placed and **truncated by the system** (no left-aligned carousel; a long title ends in an ellipsis, HIG Navigation bars: keep titles short, the system manages the title's room), while the full title stays the accessibility label. `pageChrome()` therefore installs no custom title; the left-aligned custom title (`LeadingCollapsedTitle.swift`, `leadingBarTitle`, `CollapsedTitleController`, the geometry) and the `leadingTitle` perf switch were removed in the 2026-10-28 cleanup. The header edge fade is unchanged. The folded Duo's vertical bar (`shellInVerticalBar`) keeps its system behaviour; what the journey saw there is recorded in the build digest of the 2026-10-27 run (see PROGRESS). Journeys expect `app.navigationBars.staticTexts[title]` and no `nav.title` anywhere. Gallery: `FENTON_GALLERY=shell-header` with `FENTON_GALLERY_PHONE=1` (large-title chrome), `FENTON_GALLERY_SCROLL=1`, `FENTON_GALLERY_SCROLL_TO=<row>`, `FENTON_GALLERY_TITLE=<text>`, `FENTON_GALLERY_PUSHED=1`. The [ticker](ticker.md) is no longer used for page titles. Mockup: [proposals/long-titles](../design/mockups/proposals/long-titles/long-titles.html) (2026-10-24, superseded). (3) **(Reversed the same day by (4).)** 2026-10-28, verbatim, after trying the centred collapsing title by hand on the phone: "Not good. Keep text in header the whole time." Pages have **no large title at all**: `pageChrome()` sets `.toolbarTitleDisplayMode(.inline)` on every chrome style (iPhone, iPad with or without the flyout, iPhone Duo), so the title is in the bar from the start and never collapses or moves. This **supersedes the 2026-10-20 "iPhone keeps the standard large title" rule** ([iphone](../design/apple/iphone.md), [direction-g](../design/direction-g.md), and the "large title" wording in the `.agents/pages/*/ios.md` and `ipados.md` notes, which describe the retired layout). Measured reason: Round 6 in [performance](../testing/apple/performance.md), inline titles gave 0 frames over 8 ms and 0 inset changes on Settings and Chores. The `largeTitle` and `inlineLargeTitle` perf switches that measured it were removed in the same cleanup. The fade's clear band (`HeaderEdgeFade.clearHeight`, `headerEdgeCap`) is unchanged: with an inline title the header, the smallest inset and the cap all end at the bar's bottom edge, so no title is hidden and no gap opens. (4) 2026-10-28, verbatim, once the soft scroll edge (R10) shipped: "Great. Bring back the title collapse as well." **This reverses (3):** `PageChromeTitle.mode` is `.automatic` where the title sits below the bar (iPhone, iPad with the flyout, `titleInHeader == false`), so the page has the system's large title and it collapses into the centred inline title as it scrolls (decision (1) stands: centred, the system's own, no custom title view); pushed pages inherit it, as the system does. Only `ShellChrome.titleInHeader` windows keep the inline title from the start (decision (2)), and the code makes that **only the folded Duo's vertical bar** (`ShellChrome.resolve(style: .tabBar, inVerticalBar: true)`, which has no hamburger): a compact iPad window resolves to `.tabBar` with the menu button, so it gets the large title and the `.browser` role like the iPhone, and the unfolded Duo (regular width, `.flyout`) does too. (Decision (2)'s "compact iPad" wording was written before the 2026-10-24 rule that the menu button follows the size class; the code, not that wording, is what ships.) Known cost (Round 6, performance.md): about 6 frames over 8 ms per pass on long pages as the title collapses; the owner chose the look. Journeys: `PageTitleJourneyTests` (large at rest, inline after scrolling, back at the top; pushed beside Back; named once; AX5). (5) 2026-10-28, verbatim: "Thanks. Can you left align the title again? (Still to right of hamburger)". **This reverses (1)'s centring, with the system's own API, not the custom title:** `pageChrome()` applies `.toolbarRole(.browser)` (`UINavigationItem.Style.browser`, "Title is leading aligned") wherever the hamburger shows (`chrome.showsMenuButton`: iPhone, iPad with the flyout; `PageChromeTitle.role(showsMenuButton:)`). The large title still shows at rest and collapses; the collapsed inline title sits leading, right of `nav.open` and clear of the header buttons, truncated by the system when long; a pushed page keeps Back (chevron) and the title beside it. The Duo and compact iPad keep the system default (decision (2)). `leadingBarTitle` stays unused. (6) 2026-10-29 and 2026-10-30, the iPhone Duo's vertical bar (`shellInVerticalBar`, from `toolbarVerticalEdge`). **Poses:** the folded outer display in both rotations and the unfolded inner display in landscape get the 24 pt title; the unfolded inner display in portrait has horizontal bars and keeps the iPhone's system title. **Partial landscape** (Device Hub's "Book" pose, hinge partially open, landscape): measured 2026-10-30 on the `iPhone Duo (HASS)` simulator, the window reports `toolbarVerticalEdge`, the bars sit on the trailing vertical edge (XCUITest: no tab bar, two toolbars, the same as unfolded landscape) and the app resolves `.fixedMedium`, so the title is 24 pt; the rule is geometric (the system's own vertical-bar signal), never a device or hinge-state check, so any pose that draws vertical bars gets it. The Duo's inner Split View (HIG: "In inner Split View, each app uses its outer edge") could not be produced in the simulator; the same signal decides it. **Rung 0, system API first** ([change-loop](../workflow/change-loop.md#rung-0-system-api-first)): (a) the system API is the navigation bar's own title. SwiftUI offers `toolbarTitleDisplayMode(.inline)` and, on iOS 27, `toolbarMinimizationBehavior(.never, for: .navigationBar)`; UIKit offers `UINavigationItem.attributedTitle` (iOS 26), and there is nothing before iOS 26 (the vertical bar does not exist before iOS 27.1). (b) Precedent: HIG, Designing for iPhone Duo ("navigation-focused views preserve the tab bar and move toolbar items to overflow by default; task-focused views minimize the tab bar"), and the system's own inline title. (c) Why not enough alone: on the vertical bar the system's inline title is small (about 13 pt) and its bar **minimises while a page scrolls, which takes the title with it**, and the scrolled Home keeps it hidden after a tab switch (measured 2026-10-29, base `perf-layout` @ 368d74fc, folded portrait, folded landscape and unfolded landscape: "no title anywhere"); SwiftUI has no title font API. **Owner decisions, verbatim:** option A, "Try A first" (the bar never minimises: `toolbarMinimizationBehavior(.never)`); asked whether a medium title could simply stay, "could just... stay medium"; shown 24 pt, **"Yes, 24 is the right font."**; and 2026-10-30, **"Unfolded portrait should just use the normal iPhone style title. Unfolded landscape/partial landscape should use 24pt"**. `BarTitleTreatment` (`App/Shell/VerticalBarTitle.swift`) is that decision: only `.fixedMedium` installs anything, namely the never-minimising bar and `FixedMediumTitleProbe`, a UIKit probe in the page's background that mirrors `navigationItem.title` into `attributedTitle` at 24 pt semibold (`UIFontMetrics(.title2)`, capped at 30 pt, recomputed when the text size changes), so the system still places and truncates the title and VoiceOver still reads one header. Every other window (iPhone, iPad, the Duo's unfolded portrait) resolves to `.system` and adds no view and no write: unchanged. **Rules:** (i) the page's view tree is identical in every treatment (`.background { if fixedMedium { probe } }` plus one `toolbarMinimizationBehavior` value, `.never` or `.automatic`, under an `#available` that never flips), because wrapping the page in an `if` rebuilt it, state and scroll position included, on every pose flip. (ii) On iOS 26 and later setting `attributedTitle = nil` also erases `title`, and SwiftUI does not rewrite a title that did not change, so the probe, when it goes, clears the attributed title only if it is still the string and font it wrote, and writes the plain title back; it does this from `dismantleUIViewController` (SwiftUI's guaranteed removal hook), with `willMove(toParent: nil)` as a backup, and it is idempotent. It never clears an attributed title it did not write. **Rejected:** `UINavigationBarAppearance.titleTextAttributes` (it would size every title in the bar with no UIKit hook per page, and it collides with SwiftUI's own bar-appearance writes for the soft scroll edge (R10)). **Departure from swiftui-pro api.md** ("iPhone Duo's vertical bar does not shrink"): measured on the Duo simulator the vertical bar does minimise on scroll, hence `.never`. Journeys: `DuoBarTitleJourneyTests` (24 pt at rest, after scrolling, after a tab switch, after push and Back; AX5 does not wrap; rotating unfolded landscape to portrait and back; unfolded portrait cold launch keeps the large title) and `PageTitleJourneyTests` on all three families; unit tests `VerticalBarTitleTests` and, in the iOS test bundle, `VerticalBarTitleProbeTests` (the title survives the probe going).
- **R8. (Superseded by R9, then R10, for pages on 2026-10-28.) Pages use the system's scroll-edge effect and bar material; the fade is for modals only.** Owner, 2026-10-28, verbatim: "Still choppy. Remove the fade". Asked what sits behind the title instead, the owner chose "A. iOS standard: the system's own scroll-edge effect and bar material". **This supersedes the 2026-10-13 page rule** ("Headers should not have the translucent background behind them in pages or modals. Content should fade as it scrolls up to the headers.") **for pages only.** `pageChrome()` (iOS) applies no `headerEdgeFade()` and does not hide the bar background (`toolbarBackground(.hidden)`) or the top scroll-edge effect (`scrollEdgeEffectHidden`), so R1 to R6 no longer describe pages; the page backdrop (`pageBackdrop()`) is still the navigation container's background, and the header buttons keep their glass. **Out of scope:** modals and sheets (`HassModal`, `modalChrome()`, the Weather sheet) are unchanged and still use the fade (R1 to R6) until the owner decides; the macOS legacy branch is untouched. Consumers of `headerEdgeFade()` now: `HassModal` and `WeatherSheet` only. Journeys: `HeaderFadeJourneyTests` (`testPagesHaveNoHeaderFade`, the Weather sheet's fade still follows the scroll), `ChromeJourneyTests.testCaptureChrome` (screenshots of each page top, resting and scrolled).
- **R9. (Superseded by R10 for pages.) Pages fade again under the inline title.** Owner, 2026-10-28, verbatim, after the 120 Hz scroll request shipped: "Thanks. Next, bring back fade instead of the translucent background, let's see how that does." **This reverses R8 for pages, on trial:** `pageChrome()` (iOS) applies `headerEdgeFade()` again (bar background and top scroll-edge effect hidden, content fades to clear at the bar's bottom edge), so R1 to R6 describe pages again. R7 stands: the title is the system's inline title in the bar at all times, so the clear band ends at the bar's bottom edge. Modals are unchanged (they kept the fade). **Backdrop cover (2026-10-28, the owner found Chores still struggled with the mask).** A SwiftUI `.mask` over the scrolling page makes Core Animation render the whole page offscreen every frame it scrolls: GPU and render-server cost that `tools/ios_scrollcost.sh` (main thread only) cannot see. Pages therefore use `headerEdgeFade(cover: HouseBackdrop())` (`pageChrome()`): the page backdrop is drawn again *over* the content, in the top band only, with alpha 1 minus the mask's: opaque over the header (`headerHeight`), then a ramp of `rampHeight` from `progress` (1 minus `edgeOpacity`) at the header's bottom edge to 0. Compositing is identical to the mask for opaque and translucent content. Rules: (a) the cover is laid out at the **full page size** and then framed and clipped to the band (top aligned), never laid out band-sized, because the scrim is a gradient over the page's height and the photo is shifted by the view's global origin; (b) it fills the same area as the page background (`ignoresSafeArea`), so the origins match; (c) it is `allowsHitTesting(false)` and `accessibilityHidden`; (d) at rest (progress 0) the band is a plain clipped view with no mask; while scrolled, the ramp's varying alpha is a gradient mask on the band-sized view alone, so the offscreen area is about the band (header plus 40 pt), not the page; (e) Reduce Transparency and Increase Contrast keep a ramp of 0 (hard edge), and `HouseBackdrop` already handles both. Modals (`HassModal`, `WeatherSheet`) keep the content mask (`headerEdgeFade()`): they have no backdrop. The readers, `HeaderBackgroundHidden` and the `diagnostics.headerFade` probe are unchanged.

- **R10. Pages and modals use the system's soft scroll-edge effect with the bar background hidden.** Owner, 2026-10-28, verbatim, after trying `FENTON_PAGE_EDGE=soft` on the phone: "Oh yeah, love soft. That's what we want." `pageChrome()` applies `headerSoftEdge()` (`HeaderEdgeFade.swift`): `toolbarBackgroundVisibility(.hidden, for: .navigationBar)` plus `.scrollEdgeEffectStyle(.soft, for: .top)` on iOS 26 and later. **Supersedes R8's `automatic` edge** (which on iOS 27 resolves to the more opaque `hard` look) **and R9's fade, for pages.** R9's measured reason stays as history: a full-page mask costs an offscreen pass every frame; the cover variant was built and matched the mask within 2/255 on iPhone but was dropped, with the `FENTON_PAGE_EDGE` switch. **Before iOS 26** (the deployment target is iOS 17) there is no scroll-edge effect, so hiding the bar background alone would let rows show under the title: `headerSoftEdge()` falls back there to the mask fade `headerEdgeFade()`, the closest look; callers do not check the OS. Pages on iOS 26+ install no fade probe (`diagnostics.headerFade`). **Modals follow (owner, 2026-10-28, verbatim, asked whether modals and sheets should also get it): "Yes they should use soft".** `HassModal` / `modalChrome()` and `WeatherSheet` use `headerSoftEdge()` too. `headerEdgeFade()` is now internal to HassDesign and only the pre-iOS 26 fallback. **The 2026-10-13 rule ("Headers should not have the translucent background … Content should fade as it scrolls up to the headers.") is superseded on iOS 26+ everywhere**; R1 to R6 describe the fallback only. Journey: `HeaderFadeJourneyTests.testListPageFadeFollowsTheScroll` and `testScrollViewPageFadeFollowsTheScroll` (the Weather sheet; no fade probe on iOS 26+).

HIG Scroll views: "Only use an edge effect when a scroll view is behind floating interface elements. It isn't decorative." With nothing scrolling behind the header, the effect is hidden.
