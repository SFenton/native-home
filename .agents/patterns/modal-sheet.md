# Modal sheet

> **What:** how web hash modals become native sheets. **Read when:** presenting a modal.

## Modal standard (owner, 2026-10-31)

Owner: "modals should have standards across all modals." Every modal in the app meets this checklist. One line per rule: what it is, then where it lived in the deleted SwiftUI app (kept for history; owner, 2026-11-01: the UIKit app replaces it) and where it lives in the UIKit app (`FentonHome`). A deviation is a bug in the sheet, fixed in the shared layer if it can be, and listed in the "Meets standard" column of [modal-inventory](modal-inventory.md). System alerts, confirmation dialogs, menus and pickers are not modals and are exempt.

1. **One presentation path.** SwiftUI: `.sheet` + `.hassSheet(width, height:, appearance:)` (`HassSheetPresentation`), content built on `HassModal` / `HassPathModal`. UIKit: `UKHassModalController(root:options:)` (`UKShellSheet.present` wraps it); never a hand-made `UINavigationController` or `present`. The page base is `UKPageViewController` with the sheet surface (`init(sheetTitle:)` or `context.with { $0.surface = .sheet }`): a controller that is not a page is hosted by `UKPageHost.sheetWrapped` as a safety net, and that is a deviation to port, not a design. A pane that reuses a sheet's content wraps the page, never a second copy.
2. **Width.** `HassSheetWidth` `.page` or `.form`, named by every sheet (iPad 580 x 660 form sheet, or page sheet, system placement). Duo: beside a vertical hinge (unfolded or partly folded in landscape) the sheet is the leading half (`DuoSheetPolicy.leadingHalfWidth(fold:freeWidth:)`: partly folded it ends 8 pt before the hinge's division region, flat it ends 8 pt before the midpoint of the free space, the window less the trailing rail, where the page behind divides too; owner 2026-11-01. SwiftUI passes `windowFreeWidth`, UIKit `UKDeviceSignals.freeWidth(in:)`; `.presentationPlacement(.leading)` / `preferredPlacement = .leading`). Unfolded portrait and iPhone: system width.
3. **Height and detents.** `.fitted` (content height capped at large), `.fittedToTopPage` or `.full`, never a literal. Detents come from `hassSheet` / `SheetFit` / `UKHassModalController.updateDetents` only. Folded Duo and landscape beside a vertical hinge: always full height from the bottom (`DuoSheetPolicy.fillsHeight`, geometric, no device-name check); an open sheet follows a pose change.
4. **Grabber.** Visible on a resizable sheet; hidden on a `.full` sheet elsewhere (one detent); shown by the Duo full-height rule; hidden while a draft or a save blocks dismissal (`DragIndicator.visibility`, `prefersGrabberVisible`).
5. **Close.** Circle-X at the top trailing edge, `.primaryAction`, no text "Done" (`SheetCloseItem` / `UKSheetItems.close`); on the folded Duo it is in the rail like every sheet's (`closeStaysInBar` does not apply where the full-height rule holds). A form that saves has Cancel (circle-X, leading) and Save (filled check, trailing); a pushed page keeps Close beside Back.
6. **Title and subtitle.** Inline title in the bar (never a large title), through the page's `navigationItem.title`; a subtitle only through `SheetSubtitle` / `UKSheetSubtitle` (system subtitle on iOS 26, nothing or in-content before). Title in Title Case, the same text in both apps.
7. **Background and ground.** The system grouped ground that follows the system appearance (`Surface.sheetGround`, `UKDesignSurface.systemGround`; pages stay dark on the wallpaper, sheets do not); `appearance: .dark` only over a photo, gradient or live video. Adaptive tokens only: no `.white`, `Color.black`, `Palette.*` or fixed fills on a sheet's content. A full-bleed backdrop goes through `setSheetBackdrop(_:ground:)` (UIKit) / `ignoresSafeArea` (SwiftUI).
8. **Safe areas and the rail.** Content never runs under the folded Duo's rail or any side inset; backgrounds may. UIKit: content lives in `UKPageViewController.contentView`, inset by the side safe areas. SwiftUI: native scroll content and `List` respect them.
9. **Header.** No translucent band: the system soft top edge with the bar background hidden, content scrolls under the bar and fades out under it (`modalChrome()`, [header-edge-fade](header-edge-fade.md); UIKit `UKPageViewController` header fade and `topEdgeEffect = .soft`). The scroller reaches the top of `contentView` with automatic insets (never `.never` at the safe area, which clips at the bar), and is named to the page: `install(scrollView:)` / `setPageScrollView(_:)`, or `UKSheetPinnedBarPage.setScrollers(_:)` for a content controller that builds its columns after load. A scroller the page does not know has no header fade and no scroll to top.
10. **Bottom pinned bar.** A section bar or primary action under the content is `.modalTabBar(...)` (SwiftUI) / `UKPagePinnedBar` (UIKit; `UKSheetPinnedBarPage` for a content controller): the content scrolls under it with the soft bottom edge and clears it at the end, never clips at it; the bar rides above the keyboard. No hand-made bar, no `contentInset.bottom`, no `UKPinnedBarViewController` in a sheet.
11. **Scrolling.** Only when the content outgrows the sheet (`scrollsOnlyWhenNeeded()`, `UKScrollFit`, [scroll-fit](scroll-fit.md)); tapping the status bar or the page's scroll-to-top returns to the top of the page scroller; the keyboard dismisses interactively and never covers the field in focus.
12. **Fit to content.** A fitted sheet reports its scroller's content height (`SheetFit`; UIKit `UKSheetFitting.sheetFitScrollView` on the page); one view structure in every state, so a pose change never rebuilds the page.
13. **Motion.** The standard sheet transition (no zoom out of a tile; no zoom where the full-height rule holds); in-sheet content swaps cross-fade (`UKSmoothContentSwapView`); with Reduce Motion everything fades or cuts ([motion](motion.md)).
14. **Accessibility.** The system presents the sheet as modal (the page behind is hidden from VoiceOver). VoiceOver Escape (two-finger scrub) goes back one page, else closes, asking first when the form is guarded (`UKHassModalController.accessibilityPerformEscape`; SwiftUI sheets get it from the system). Close is named "Close", Cancel and Save are named, every target is 44 pt or more, the title is a header, identifiers are stable. Initial focus lands on the title; closing returns focus to the opener. Works at AX5.
15. **Discard guard.** A form that holds user-generated content asks before Cancel, Back, Close or a swipe down drops an edited draft (`DiscardChangesCopy`; `SheetCancelItem(hasChanges:)` / `.discardGuard`, UIKit `UKSheetItems.cancel(hasChanges:)` and `UKDiscardGuard.apply`). Selection-only sheets are not guarded.

- Web hash routes (`#lights-overview`) map to a `HomeSheet` enum driving `.sheet(item:)`. The URL hash is not needed natively.
- Container: `HassDesign/HassModal.swift`. **Every sheet is built on it** (owner, 2026-10-26, `hass-modal`): `HassModal(title, closeIdentifier:) { content }` for one page; `HassPathModal(title, path:) { root } page: { $0.navigationDestination(...) }` for a sheet that pushes pages. `page:` receives the chromed page (`ModalPage`), so a section bar (`modalTabBar`), a subtitle, a destination or a `.toolbar` (a Cancel and Save form passes `showsClose: false`) goes on the page and never around the container; other parameters: `closeEnabled`, `closeStaysInBar`, `appearance: .dark` (live video), `title: nil` when the page sets its own. Do not assemble `NavigationStack` + `modalChrome()` + `SheetCloseItem` + `followsSystemAppearance()` by hand. Exempt: the Weather sheet (its own photo backdrop and header fade, no `modalChrome`) and pages pushed inside a sheet, which carry `SheetCloseItem` themselves.
- Close: the circle-X `SheetCloseItem` at the top trailing edge (`Button(role: .close)` on iOS 26), never a text "Done" or "Close" (owner, 2026-10-13, matching FestivalNativeApps; HIG Toolbars: "Close dismisses a modal; prefer their standard symbols without text labels"). A sheet that saves keeps its Save or Add button; Cancel stays only where it discards an edit.
- **Close chrome (owner, 2026-10-24, `proposals/sheet-close-chrome`, option C).** `SheetCloseItem` sits in `.primaryAction`, not `.confirmationAction`: Close confirms nothing, and on the iPhone Duo's vertical bar a confirming slot sorts as the prominent action below Back. `.primaryAction` is still the trailing edge on iPhone and iPad, so the top-right rule stands. A sheet whose only toolbar item is Close (`HassModal`, Security System, the camera viewer) adds `.keepsCloseInSheetBar()` (`toolbarVerticalBehavior(.disabled)`, iOS 27.1 and later) so the Duo keeps it in the sheet's own bar; pending a Duo device run. **`.hassSheet(_, height: .full)` hides the grabber** (`DragIndicator.visibility(for:)`): one detent, nothing to resize; swipe down still dismisses. The other hand-built Close users (`OptionPickerSheet` now builds on `HassModal`) share the one item.
- **Discard guard (owner, 2026-10-24, `proposals/sheet-discard-guard`, option A).** A form sheet that holds user-generated content asks before Cancel, Back, Close or a swipe down drops an edited draft: title "Discard changes?", destructive "Discard Changes", cancel "Keep Editing" (`DiscardChangesCopy`, in `HassDesign/DiscardGuard.swift`; the alerts work folds it into `AlertCopy`). Saving stays the confirming action. Build: `SheetCancelItem(hasChanges:)`, `SheetCloseItem(hasChanges:)` and `SheetBackItem(hasChanges:isEnabled:)` (a pushed form's Back; `.hidesSystemBack()` turns off the system Back and its edge swipe, the one route a guard cannot intercept) show a `.confirmationDialog` attached to their own button (a popover anchored to it on iPad), and `.discardGuard(hasChanges:isBusy:)` on the sheet root blocks the swipe down while the draft has changes or a save runs (`DiscardGuard.blocksInteractiveDismissal`). A clean form closes at once, by every route. Guarded: the Choreapp task form, add-admin-todo, rename-todo, the inventory item editor, scan item (`ScanItemModel.hasDraft`), the bed alarm, wake light and humidifier activity editors, and the sprinkler schedule page (Back **and** Close). Not guarded, because one tap redoes them: selection-only sheets (recipe and inventory filter and sort, the solo trip editor, the vacation confirmation). `dismissalConfirmationDialog` (iOS 27) is not used: its dismiss semantics are unverified on a device, so the swipe is blocked rather than intercepted.
- **One sheet at a time, Close at every depth (owner, 2026-10-24, `proposals/sheet-stacking-close`).** Never present a sheet from inside a sheet. A pushed page carries Close beside the system Back (Quick Links Rooms and Security System, the Home Assistant web page, the sprinkler pages; the household sheets already did). A two-choice question is a `.confirmationDialog` on its trigger (the bed temperature scope, Tonight / All Nights, on the dial), not a second sheet. A destination reached from a row inside a sheet is a **pushed page** (`isPushed`): Share Location (`LocationPermissionView`) and Edit Task (`DonetickTaskSheet`) from Your Summary push inside its stack, keep the summary behind them, and use the system or guarded Back instead of Cancel. The after-sign-in location offer is not inside a sheet, so it stays a page sheet. A pushed Share Location can be left by Back even before the first prompt; the "no other way out" pre-alert rule is for the unprompted offer.
- Create and edit sheets (direction G, Sheets): `SheetCancelItem` (circle x, leading) and `SheetConfirmItem("Save", isEnabled:isBusy:)` (a check on a filled `#0071E3` circle, trailing, with an inline `ArcSpinner` while saving), both in `HassDesign/SheetEditToolbar.swift`, as in Calendar and Reminders. Never text Cancel/Save buttons and never a second copy of these. **The check is visible and disabled until there is a change** (owner, 2026-10-16; `SheetEditState.canConfirm(isValid:hasChanges:isBusy:)`): a new item needs a valid draft, an edit also needs a difference from what is saved, and a save in flight shows the spinner. It is dimmed when disabled and filled `#0071E3` when enabled; never hide it. Sheets still on their own text Save/Submit buttons (the humidifier schedule editor, the feedback form, the bed and wake-light editors' in-page buttons) are known gaps.
- Header: no translucent band; the system's soft scroll-edge effect with the bar background hidden, as on pages (owner, 2026-10-28, R10; a mask fade before iOS 26) (`modalChrome()`, [header-edge-fade](header-edge-fade.md)). Each page pushed inside a modal applies `modalChrome()` too, and so does every sheet that builds its own `NavigationStack` (`FilterSheet` did not until 2026-10-16, which left the system's translucent bar behind "Filter Recipes"). Modals have no house backdrop ([page-backdrop](page-backdrop.md)).
- **Height (owner, 2026-10-16; replaces 2026-10-13's "all modals full height on iPhone").** `.hassSheet()` is **content-fitted** by default: `modalChrome()` reports the page's scroll content height (`onScrollGeometryChange`: content plus the header and any bottom inset beyond the bottom safe area, from the page's own vertical scroller, iOS 18 and later; HassDesign `SheetFit.swift`) to the sheet, which sets a `.height` detent capped at large (`SheetFit.detent`), with the grabber visible. **The detent leaves out the bottom safe area (owner, 2026-10-28: option B, "fit to content"; proposal `proposals/sheet-bottom-gap/`, [decision](../design/sheet-bottom-gap.md)).** UIKit measures a custom detent up from the bottom safe area and adds that area itself, so a detent `D` is a sheet `D` plus the safe area tall (34 pt on iPhone and the folded Duo, 20 pt on iPad); asking for the safe area too, as the fit did until then, left that much empty ground under the last control. `SheetFit.wantedHeight(contentHeight:topInset:bottomInset:systemBottomInset:)` counts only `SheetFit.bottomBeyondSafeArea(inset:safeArea:)` (a pinned bar's height stays; the home indicator does not), with `systemBottomInset` the window's bottom safe area (`LargeDetent.bottomSafeArea()`, set on the model by `FittedSheet`); every fitted sheet on iPhone, the folded Duo and iPad portrait is that much shorter. **iPad:** its portrait form and page sheets are custom-detent sheets too (a 436 detent was a 456 pt hosting view), so the same rule applies there; in landscape the centred sheet's page reads a bottom safe area of 0, so nothing changes. A sheet never has a second band under the safe area: `SheetBottomGapJourneyTests` asserts the last control ends within 72 pt of the window's bottom edge and that the page's scroller holds no more viewport than content (`FENTON_SHEETFIT_LEGACY=1`, Debug, draws the old detent to measure the same build both ways). It opens at `.medium` only the first time on a device (before there is a measurement) unless the page predicts its content height (`predictedContentHeight`, below), at the last measured height after that (remembered per call site by `fitKey`, default `#fileID:#line`, **persisted in UserDefaults** across launches under a context of app build, window height and text size: `SheetFitMemory`). Remembered heights and chrome are cleared at sign-out (`SheetMemory.clearAll()`, called by `AppSession.signOut()`; owner, 2026-10-26: "we clear everything on sign-out"); the cost is one measured open per sheet on a regular-width device after the next sign-in (compact width predicts anyway). A fitted sheet taller than half the window also offers `.large` as a second detent (`SheetFit.offersLarge`) so people can expand it; the fitted height stays the smallest detent, so it still opens there and the content never asks for large (an unmeasured sheet is `[.medium, .large]`, so iOS 17, which never measures, can expand too), and moves only for changes of 2 pt or more. The first page to report owns the height, so pushing a page inside the sheet does not resize it. **Phone sheets fit to content plus the header only (the bottom safe area is the system's)** (`SheetFitModel.usesSheetEstimate`, set from the horizontal size class): the second estimate (sheet height minus the scroller's viewport plus its content) is for an iPad scroller laid out below the header and is skipped at compact width, because a scroller that does not grow with the sheet reads one viewport at every height, so each taller sheet asked for 14 pt more until the sheet reached large (the bathroom fan sheet kept 400 pt of empty ground, 2026-10-17). iOS draws an inset (non-large) sheet's content at about 0.955x on iPhone (384 of 402 pt), so a 44 pt control in a fitted sheet measures 42 pt; journeys allow 41.5 for those. On iPad the form or page sheet is fitted to the same height (`presentationSizing(.form.fitted(...))` with the measured ideal height). **`HassSheetSizing` keeps one view structure in every state** (2026-10-23): `frame(idealHeight:)` takes an optional (nil until measured) and only the sizing value changes. It used to switch between two `if` branches at the first measurement; a different branch is a different view identity, so the sheet's page was rebuilt, its `SheetFitModel` page id changed, and every later reading was ignored (the model only accepts the first reporter, so a pushed page cannot resize the sheet). The sheet kept the first, stale height. **An iPad page or form sheet does not resize to a better answer after it opens, so the first layout and the first measurement must already be the final ones** (the camera viewer's split row measured 468 pt at the full window width before the sheet had its 928 pt width, against 362 pt settled: a 532 pt sheet with 106 pt of empty ground). `.hassSheet(_, height: .full)` opens at large and is **required** for: a remote (media remote), any modal with a section tab bar, recipe detail (full screen), a sheet that pushes pages (Account, room card, room overview, Sprinklers, the presence override) and a gradient or a live scanner (Weather, the scanner). A new sheet states its height in review: fitted unless one of those applies. **`.fittedToTopPage` (owner, 2026-10-26, Quick Links: "it should only show what it has to on any device").** A sheet whose pages are short and differ in length lets the page on top own the height: `SheetFitModel(followsTopPage:)` keeps each page's last reading, the reporter's `onAppear` hands ownership to the page that appeared last, so a pushed page fits the sheet to itself and Back returns to the root's height at once; only the root's heights are remembered. A pushed page that reports nothing keeps the height. An iPad page or form sheet does not resize after it opens, so there the root's height stays and a longer page scrolls. Quick Links is the only user; it left the `.full` list above. It is fitted, so on iOS 26 it also presents like the camera viewer: out of its header button (`QuickLinksZoom`, a `matchedTransitionSource` on the bar item, iOS 26 only; not in the Duo's rail, where the button shares Profile's glass group). `FilterSheet(height:)` follows the same rule and keys its memory by title.
- **Scrolling (owner, 2026-10-24).** `modalChrome()` also applies `scrollsOnlyWhenNeeded()`: a sheet whose content fits is static (no bounce, no indicator) and scrolls only once the content outgrows it, including a fitted sheet capped at large. Rule, exception for pull-to-refresh and tests: [scroll-fit](scroll-fit.md).
- **Camera viewer (owner, 2026-10-23; replaces its `.full`).** The viewer is fitted like Security System: it has one page and never pushes or tabs, so only the video and its controls set the height (about half the screen on iPhone). Live video is not a reason for full height; it only makes the sheet dark (`appearance: .dark`). Both layouts (stacked, and video beside controls on iPad landscape) sit in one page scroller so `SheetFit` can measure either. Measured (2026-10-23, before the safe area left the detent: it was 34 pt more on iPhone and the folded Duo, 20 on iPad): iPhone Front Door about 500 pt (74 chrome + 392 content + 34 safe area; iOS 26.5, opaque ground, nothing shows through); iPad portrait about 740 pt; iPad landscape 426 pt (64 + 362, split row); folded iPhone Duo at large (the large detent is only about 426 pt there, so it scrolls by design). Window metrics are read at creation (`WindowMetrics.current`) and the split halves are flexible, so the first layout is the final height. **Computed before it opens, so even the first open is final (owner, 2026-10-26: "Why does first open have to measure and move at all? Can't we precompute this?").** Where the sheet's width is known before it opens (compact width: iPhone and the folded Duo, where it is the window's width, `WindowMetrics.current`), the viewer passes `hassSheet(predictedContentHeight:)`: the stacked layout (`CameraViewerStackedLayout`: video over `CameraControlsLayout`, the same views the real viewer lays out, so nothing is copied) is laid out off screen with `OffscreenLayout.size(of:width:typeSize:)` (a hosting controller never added to a window, so no stream starts and no state is written), with `Color.clear.cameraVideoFrame(camera)` where the stream plays (video = width over the config's `aspectRatio`) and the controls in their idle state at the current text size (they stack in a column at AX5). The sheet opens at `chrome.top + predicted + chrome.bottom`, rounded (the bottom is only what lies beyond the safe area: 0), and that **takes precedence over a remembered height**; a measurement still corrects a wrong prediction (2 pt hysteresis unchanged). The chrome is `SheetChromeMemory` (HassDesign `SheetChrome.swift`; stored as `sheetChrome.v2`, heights as `sheetFit.heights.v2`: the 2026-10-28 rule changed what a stored value means, so the `v1` tables, whose chrome bottom was 34 and whose heights counted the safe area, are never read and are deleted at launch and by `SheetMemory.clearAll()`): learned from **any** fitted `HassModal` whose root page has the standard inline header (`SheetFitModel.rootChrome`; a page with a pinned bar, `modalTabBar` either placement, is excluded through `hasCustomSheetHeader()`), kept in UserDefaults under a context **without the build** (system version, large-detent height, text size, width class), so a new build does not forget it. The chrome also carries `widthInset` (window width less the page's width: 0 on iPhone, where the page lays out at the window's width and iOS draws the sheet scaled; 16 on the folded Duo, iOS 27.1, where the page is laid out at the sheet's inset width), so `SheetMemory.pageWidth(inWindowOfWidth:)` gives the width to predict at. **No warm-up (owner, 2026-10-26).** None of the chrome is computable: UIKit decides it when it presents. A hidden warm-up sheet that taught it before anyone opened one was tried and dropped; the rule is now: **the very first sheet ever opened in a context (after install, sign-out or a text-size change) may correct once, by a few points, while it learns; every later sheet opens at its final height with zero moves**, on iPhone and the folded Duo. Measured with `FENTON_SHEETFIT_PROBE=1` (the probe string also carries the presented view's ancestors, frames, transforms and the presentation container, and `chrome=[...]`: what the model read, `cut`, the sheet's page area and what `SheetChromeMemory` holds for the context): on the **iPhone 17 Pro** (402 x 874, iOS 26.5 and 27.0) the page lays out at the window's 402 pt inside a `UIDropShadowView` scaled 0.960, header 70, bottom 34 (the safe area, now the system's); on the **folded iPhone Duo** (466 x 678, iOS 27.1) the sheet is **not scaled** and the page lays out at 450 pt (8 pt inset a side), header 74 in a 369, 479 or 557 pt sheet alike. In both a custom detent `D` gives a sheet `D + 34` tall (the detent is measured up from the bottom safe area; the sheet then floats 8 pt above the window's bottom), which is why the detent no longer asks for the 34 pt itself (owner, 2026-10-28: B, fit to content; `proposals/sheet-bottom-gap/`; it used to leave 34 pt of empty ground under the last control). **The page area (`SheetFitModel.setSheetHeight`) is the sheet less the safe area**: a sheet that got its detent `D` reads exactly `D` (Upper Deck 445 in a 479 pt sheet; it was 479 in a 513), the large detent reads 554 on the Duo (a 588 pt sheet, presented from y 90), and `maxSheetHeight` is in this measure (unchanged by the 2026-10-28 rule, so it keeps its key). A sheet is *cut short* when its page area is less than the detent it asked for (`isCutShort`): the Duo's Front Door asked 565 before 2026-10-28, the system pinned it at 554 and 8 pt higher (y 82), and its page's header then read **82**, not 74 (lab sheets of 620 and 628 flipped between 74 and 82 and moved 22 times); it now asks 523, which the 554 pt page area holds, so it opens at y 113 with a 74 header, uncut (the proposal's estimate, 531, used the pinned 82). Content that outgrows the page area (AX5, a lab sheet) is still cut short and handled the same way. A cut-short sheet ignores a taller reading (it cannot grow), teaches the bottom inset and width but keeps the top it knew, and a sheet whose detent is within a point of `maxSheetHeight` does the same. **Root cause of the "first sheet teaches nothing" bug (2026-10-26, after the warm-up was dropped):** `isCutShort` compared the page area with `detent + bottom inset`, as if the page area included the inset, so every ordinary sheet (Driveway 370 against 404) read as cut short, `rootChrome` stayed nil, nothing was ever learned except from a large sheet (which the warm-up supplied), and the second camera (Upper Deck) opened from the fallback chrome at 484 and moved to 479. Unit tests had used the wrong measure (403 for a 369 detent), so they passed. Fallback before anything is learned: bottom 0 (no inset beyond the safe area), width inset 0, top = `SheetChromeDefaults` (**70**; **74** where the window has no top safe area, the Duo), so the first sheet can be a few points off once (Duo: Driveway 340 then 335, Front Door 535 then 523; the first measurement corrects it). Journeys (probe `initial / final / moves`, re-measured 2026-10-28 after the safe area left the detent; the numbers in brackets were the old detents), Duo, everything reset: Driveway **340/335/1** (first-ever, was 374/369/1), then Upper and Lower Deck 445/445/0 (479), Front Door 523/523/0 (557, which the system pinned at 554 and cut short; it now fits uncut at y 113), Driveway again 335/335/0 (369); first-ever Front Door 535/523/1 (569/565/1) then Driveway 335/335/0; AX5 Front Door 1058/1046/0 and Driveway 858/858/0 (both at large). iPhone 17 Pro (iOS 26.5 and 27.0): Front Door 483/483/0 (517), Driveway 317/317/0 (351; also as the very first sheet), Upper and Lower Deck 414/414/0 (448), AX5 Front Door 1006 and Driveway 839, all 0 (the fallback header, 70, is already right there, and the fallback bottom is now 0 like the learned one, so even the first sheet needs no correction). iPad Pro 11 portrait (iOS 27.0, regular width, nothing predicted): Driveway 416 (436), Upper and Lower Deck 605 (625), Front Door 740 (760), with the measured path's usual moves while the sheet presents (2 to 16, the same count as before). iPad landscape is unchanged at 426 (the page reads a bottom safe area of 0). The last control ends 56 pt above the window's bottom edge on an iPhone (it was 89), 58 on the Duo (92) and 44 on an iPad (63). **A predicted sheet does not follow a reading at once**: the page lays out at transient sizes while the sheet presents (Front Door read 569, 517, 551, 547, 530 ... 518 on its way to 517), so a reading must stand still 300 ms (`SheetFit.settleDelay`) before it moves a sheet that opened at a predicted height; a reading within the 2 pt hysteresis of the height cancels the wait. **Regular width (iPad, unfolded Duo page sheet) predicts nothing**: the sheet's width is not known until it opens, so there the sheet opens at the camera's own remembered height (`fitKey: "CameraViewer.<entity id>"`, one per camera: Driveway, 10:3, is not Front Door, 4:3) and only the very first open of a camera in a build, window size and text size measures after it appears. The split layout predicts nothing either (the controls can be the taller half). `FENTON_SHEETFIT_RESET=1` (Debug) clears both tables at launch so a journey can see the first open; `CameraViewerFitJourneyTests` covers all three families (and `testFirstOpenIsAtTheFinalHeight`: reset, open Front Door and Driveway, the sheet frame equal after the presentation and 1.5 s later; and `testEveryCameraOpensAtItsFinalHeight`: Driveway, Upper Deck, Lower Deck, Front Door, Driveway in turn; the first sheet of the run may move once, every later probe is `moves=0`, on iPhone and Duo); atlas mockup `.agents/design/mockups/modals/cameras/camera-viewer/` (the decision: `proposals/camera-viewer-fit/`).
- **iPad size (plan Phase 4, 2026-10-15).** Every sheet names it: `.hassSheet(.page)` for viewing and control sheets with tile grids, dials, maps or a second column (chip sheets, Security System, Weather, Quick Links, room card sheets, Sprinklers, Custom Light, remotes, thermostat, vacuum, recipe detail, camera viewer); `.hassSheet(.form)` for lists, editors and prompts (light mode picker, Vacation and Solo Trip editors, Recipes filters, Bed temperature scope, Add Item, todo editors, filter and sort sheets). `HassSheetWidth` has two cases and the default is `.form`: there is no `.automatic`, so a sheet that forgets still gets a narrow readable sheet instead of the system's default, and the rule above decides when to ask for `.page`. `hassSheetSizing(_:)` stays for a sheet that must manage its own detents; none does today. A sheet that bypasses `hassSheet` for its own reason (the item editor's grabber that hides while a save blocks dismissal) still routes through it and overrides the one modifier after.
- **Duo sheet placement (owner, 2026-10-31: "Modals still opening centered when unfolded").** On a device with a hinge every sheet and modal asks the system for the leading placement: UIKit `sheetPresentationController.preferredPlacement = .leading` (iOS 27, ignored when `sourceView` is set, so a sheet never sets one), SwiftUI `.presentationPlacement(.leading)` on the sheet content (inside `hassSheet`). Unfolded in landscape that opens it against the left edge instead of centred; partly folded the system already keeps it in the left half; folded, iPhone and iPad are unaffected (iPad keeps `.automatic`: centred). Sources: sarunw.com/posts/presentation-placement-on-iphone-duo/ and the iOS 27.1 SDK headers. UIKit: the one call is `UKSheetPlacement.place(_:in:)`, made by every sheet path (`UKHassModalController`, `UKShellSheet`, the header-sheet path); a new presentation site calls it too. Never set a sheet's width or frame. See [hinge-avoidance](hinge-avoidance.md).
- **Full-height sheets on the folded and landscape Duo (owner, 2026-10-31: "On duo landscape or folded, modals can always just open full height and from the bottom").** One geometric rule, `DuoSheetPolicy.fillsHeight(hasHinge:hinge:)` (`HassDesign/DuoSheetPolicy.swift`), read by both apps: it holds when the window has a hinge (`HingePresence.hasHinge`, mirrored to `DuoSheetPolicy.deviceHasHinge`) and either no division region is visible (the folded outer screen, `windowHinge == nil`) or the hinge band is vertical (height > width: unfolded or partly folded in landscape). Never a device-name or pose check. There every sheet and modal takes the **large detent only** (no content-fitted or medium detent, no resize between pages, `fittedToTopPage` included), rises from the bottom with the **standard sheet transition (no zoom out of a source tile**: the camera viewer from a Home or Security tile, and Quick Links out of its header button), **keeps the grabber** (a `.full` sheet's grabber is hidden elsewhere; here it shows) and keeps swipe to dismiss. A fitted sheet keeps measuring underneath, so it fits again when the rule stops applying. **Unchanged:** unfolded portrait (a horizontal band), iPhone and iPad. **On a pose change** an open sheet moves to the right detent (fold, unfold, rotate). **Fixed-height and short sheets (decision):** nothing opens at a small fixed height (no `.height(n)` literals; fitted prompts, pickers and short editors are content-fitted), so the rule simply overrides the fit for those too: HIG Sheets asks for the smallest detent that shows the content, but on the folded outer screen (a narrow, tall display) and beside the hinge in landscape a partial sheet leaves a thin strip of the page and a sheet that jumps height between poses, so one predictable full-height presentation wins; the content keeps its natural layout at the top of the sheet. Alerts, confirmation dialogs and popovers are not sheets and are not affected. **UIKit:** `UKSheetPlacement.fillsHeight(in:)` feeds `UKHassModalController` (`fillsHeight`: `updateDetents` takes `[.large]`, grabber, drops `preferredTransition`), `UKShellSheet` and the header-sheet path (`UKSectionNavigationController.presentHeaderSheet`); an open sheet follows a pose change: `UKHassModalController` and `UKSheetNavigationController` check `UKSheetPlacement.PoseWatch` in `viewDidLayoutSubviews` and re-run the sheet settings inside `animateChanges`; the camera viewer sets no zoom `preferredTransition`. `UKDeviceSignals.hinge(in:)` falls back to the window's root view when a view reports no hinge (a flat Duo reports its inactive hinge to the app's views, not to a window or a sheet's transition view; measured 2026-10-31). **SwiftUI:** `hassSheet` (`HassSheetPresentation`) reads `windowHinge` and passes `fillsHeight` to `SheetHeightModifier`/`FittedSheet` (one view structure in every state, so a fold never rebuilds the page), shows the drag indicator and stops fitting the iPad ideal height; `zoomDestination` skips `navigationTransition(.zoom)`. A new presentation site goes through these, never a detent list of its own.
- Appearance (owner decision, 2026-10-15): pages stay dark on the wallpaper, sheets follow the system appearance. `hassSheet()` and `HassModal` apply `followsSystemAppearance()` (the scheme `RootView` publishes as `systemColorScheme`, because the shell's `preferredColorScheme(.dark)` hides it from the environment); a sheet over a photo or gradient says `hassSheet(appearance: .dark)` (Weather, the camera viewer). ## One commit model per form (owner, 2026-10-24)

A form either keeps a draft for everything it shows (Save or Apply writes it, Cancel or Back drops it, and leaving with edits asks via `DiscardGuard`) or says which controls act at once. Do not mix them silently (HIG Entering data).

- **Inventory item editor:** Quantity, Expiration and Prepared Food are drafts (`InventoryEditorModel.quantityDrafts`, `expiryDrafts`, `preparedDrafts`), written by Save in that order (`savePrepared` last, finding its batch again by row ids because an expiry change re-keys it). Delete is in its own group with a footer; it asks first and applies at once.
- **Sprinkler program page:** "Right now" (Program Enabled, Start Program Now) applies immediately, since turning a program off is expected to take effect; "Schedule" (days, start times, budget) waits for Apply. Each group's footer says so.
- **Number pad:** a field with a number or decimal pad that has no return key gets `keyboardDone(focused:)` (a Done above the keyboard that clears focus and so commits the field).
- **Visible labels:** a single-field form still gets a `FormHeader` naming the field (Add Grocery Item "Item", Add Task "Task"); the placeholder is not the label.

- **Owned files** (also in `contracts/patterns.json`): `KeyboardDone.swift`, `SheetSubtitle.swift`, `BadgedTabSegment.swift`, `BadgedTabSegments.swift`.

## Safe area and the rail (owner, 2026-10-31)

- **Content never runs under the folded Duo's rail** (or any side inset). UIKit: **every page in a sheet is a `UKPageViewController`** (sheet surface, `context.surface = .sheet`), whose `contentView` is inset by the side safe areas; the page's content goes in `contentView` (`install(scrollView:)` for a scroller). A page's own full-bleed ground (the weather gradient) goes through `setSheetBackdrop(_:ground:)`, behind `contentView`, so it runs under the rail while the content does not. Anything that is not a page (a system or web view, a controller not yet ported) is hosted: `UKHassModalController` wraps every root, pushed and set controller in `UKPageHost.sheetWrapped(_:)` (title, bar items and `UKSheetFitting` forwarded; the hosted scroll view is left alone), so nothing can bypass the inset. There is no runtime constraint rewriting (`UKSheetSafeArea` and `ukFullBleed` are gone). SwiftUI scroll content already respects the inset; backgrounds use `ignoresSafeArea` only.
- **A bar pinned under a sheet page (owner, 2026-10-31): one component, `UKPagePinnedBar`.** A section bar (`UKModalTabBar`) or one primary action pinned along the bottom of a sheet page is `UKPagePinnedBar` (`Design/UKPagePinnedBar.swift`): the page calls `install(in:)` in `viewDidLoad` and `layoutChanged()` in `viewDidLayoutSubviews`. The bar sits in the page's `view` (outside the header-fade mask), rests on the system's bottom safe area and rides above the keyboard; its height joins the page's `additionalSafeAreaInsets` so content scrolls clear of it. It is pinned to the view's bottom and the keyboard guide with `usesBottomSafeArea = false`, never to a guide that includes the page's own additional inset (the bar would chase its own inset and rise). With `scrollView` it takes the iOS 26 scroll edge interaction and the scroll view a soft edge; `barMaterial` is the pre-iOS 26 bar material. A page that owns its content (media remote, Your Summary) uses it directly; a sheet whose content is its own controller (bed, wake light, humidifier, climate, device accessory, sprinkler overview, water and schedule) hosts it in `UKSheetPinnedBarPage(content:bar:scrollView:barMaterial:)`, which only places the child in `contentView`, names its scroller and owns a `UKPagePinnedBar`. `UKPinnedBarViewController` remains for non-sheet pages and the design galleries; do not use it for a sheet page.
- **`closeStaysInBar` does not apply where every sheet fills the height.** Disabling the vertical bar (`toolbarVerticalBehavior(.disabled)`, `preferredVerticalBarBehavior`) makes the system restore the status bar across the top of the folded outer screen and seat the sheet below it, so the camera viewer, Security System and App Icon sheets stopped about 90 pt short of the top while every other sheet reached it. Folded and in landscape beside the hinge the rule is full height from the bottom, so Close sits in the rail like any other sheet's (`CloseInBar`, `UKHassModalController.preferredVerticalBarBehavior`). Unfolded portrait still keeps Close in the bar.
- **A folded Duo is recognised even before the shell has seen a hinge change:** `UKSheetPlacement.hasHinge` also reads `verticalBarEdge` from the presenter's view.
- One UIKit path: `UKShellSheet.present` builds a `UKHassModalController`. Inventory of every modal: [modal-inventory](modal-inventory.md).
