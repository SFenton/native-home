# iPadOS

> **What:** iPad navigation decisions. **Read when:** building for regular width.

- Navigation: the system tab bar at the top, in the navigation bar's row. One `UITabBarController` in mode `.tabBar` (never the sidebar) shows in every window and is never hidden for style; the system places it from the size class (HIG, Tab bars, iPadOS: "The bar is near the top"), so the app has no placement code. `UKServerUIRenderer.updateStyle()` reads `ShellNavigationStyle.resolve(isRegularWidth:hasHinge:)`:
  - Regular width without a hinge (iPad, any orientation) is `.topTabBar`: no menu button and no flyout edge swipe, because a second navigator in the row would repeat the first. The collapsed title leads the row (`ShellChrome.titleLeads`, `UINavigationItem.Style.browser`) because the tabs hold the centre (HIG, Toolbars). The large title stays below the bar. The row reads Back or Close, the title, the tabs, Quick Links, Profile.
  - Compact width (iPhone, folded Duo, a compact iPad window) keeps the bottom tab bar, the menu button and the flyout, and so does a window with a hinge, the unfolded Duo. The style follows the size class and the hinge only, never the device idiom, so rotation never swaps the navigation model.
  - The flyout's rules below apply wherever the menu button shows.
  - Rows follow the web rail/drawer (`AdaptiveNavigation.tsx`): heading "Navigation", Home, Security, Climate, Chores, Settings (the same five as the tab bar). Quick Links is not a row: it is a header button. Only Chores carries a badge (the shell's attention count, web `BADGED_NAV_PATH`), spoken as "Chores, N overdue".
  - Closes on a destination, scrim tap, Escape (`.cancelAction`) or the VoiceOver escape gesture. Opening focuses the heading; closing without a destination returns focus to Open Navigation.
  - The content is hidden from VoiceOver while it is open, on three layers (verified on iPad 2026-10-11; one layer alone left elements in the audit): `accessibilityHidden(while:)` on the `TabView`, the same on every page through `.pageChrome()` (SwiftUI vends a page's elements past UIKit), and `accessibilityElementsHidden` on the root `UITabBarController` view (the UIKit navigation bar's large title is out of SwiftUI's reach). Use `accessibilityHidden(while:)` (`isEnabled:` form), never `.accessibilityHidden(bool)`.
  - The scrim is two layers: a full-window dim that neither hit-tests nor reaches VoiceOver, and the **Close Navigation** target (`nav.flyout.scrim`) beside the panel only. A full-window close button won the accessibility hit test over the rows. The dim covers the whole window, the status bar area included (owner, 2026-10-22: the top safe area must darken like the body); the Close target still stops at the safe area.
  - Under both layers sits `NavigationFlyoutTouchShield`: a full-window, invisible, VoiceOver-hidden layer that takes every tap and click the panel and Close target do not (the status bar strip, the page behind), so no card, weather card included, answers while the flyout is open. A tap on it closes the flyout. It stays up until the close animation ends (`isSettling`), so a tap during the slide-out is swallowed too (owner, 2026-10-27). Journey: `testTapsOutsideOpenFlyoutOnlyCloseIt`.
  - The selected row is primary ink on an accent tint (`accentColor` at 18%), like Settings' selected row; white on the accent read 2.5:1. Rows have vertical padding so they grow with Dynamic Type.
  - The leading-edge swipe (on every window that shows the menu button, iPhone included) is a `UIScreenEdgePanGestureRecognizer` on the renderer. Once it recognizes it cancels the touch in the content, so a swipe that starts on a control opens only the flyout; a SwiftUI simultaneous drag also opened the Home camera tile under it on the landscape Duo. Never a hit-testable edge strip: it swallowed taps on Open Navigation and on row edges.
  - On the unfolded Duo (a window with a hinge) the tab bar stays and the system draws it in its trailing vertical bar, icons only, with the header group above it and no hamburger (observed on the simulator, unfolded landscape, 2026-10-26); the bar covers the trailing ~83 pt (taps there never reach the app).
  - Departure from the web: the web shows a persistent 248 px rail at ≥1120×820; the native app shows the system tab bar at the top, with no persistent rail or sidebar. Five sections are too few for sidebar conversion (HIG, Tab bars, iPadOS).
- Rotates freely. Portrait is always single page; landscape may split.
- Splits follow [split-view.md](split-view.md): on demand, landscape regular width only, 50/50 at the midpoint.
- Grids grow columns with width through `DynamicGridLayout` (web UX contract). Resolved 2026-10-15 (plan Phase 4, decision 1): direction G's "4 columns" is a floor, not a ceiling. A full-width tile grid reaches at least 4 columns at iPad Pro 11 portrait (content about 802 pt) and keeps growing; caps of 3 on tile grids are gone (Chores, Vacuums, Household settings), and the one remaining bound is Chores quick links at 4 (five links, one two tracks wide). Room pages and Media show 2 + 2 tiles across by laying their sections side by side. See [dynamic-grid](../../patterns/dynamic-grid.md).
- Status chips (Home, Security, room pages) wrap onto rows in regular width and scroll sideways in compact width (`ChipRail`, [status-rail](../../patterns/status-rail.md)); nothing in regular width hides behind a scroll.
- **Quick Links and Profile are header buttons** on every page in every window (owner, 2026-10-16, reversing the floating 56 pt circle of 2026-10-15, #84): two trailing glass circles, Quick Links then Profile, on section roots and pushed pages alike (`shellHeaderButtons()` from `pageChrome()`; the sheets hang once per section stack, `shellSheets(caller:)`). `FloatingQuickLinksButton` and the flyout's Quick Links row are gone; `quickLinks.open` and `nav.profile` identify the circles on every device. On iPad the large title stays below the bar and the collapsed title leads the row, with the tabs in the centre and no hamburger; on the folded Duo it shares the row ([direction-g](../direction-g.md), Navigation).
- **Sheets name their size.** `.hassSheet(.page)` for viewing and control sheets with tile grids, dials, maps or a second column; `.hassSheet(.form)` for lists, editors and prompts; the default is `.form`. There is no `.automatic`. HIG: Sheets, "Prefer page or form sheet styles in an iPadOS app". See [modal-sheet](../../patterns/modal-sheet.md).
