Weather card: the disabled Previous and Next chevrons

At either end of the hourly strip, the chevron that can't page any further fades to 1.9:1 and fails the accessibility audit. This proposal offers three ways to make it read at 3:1 or better, or to remove it.

The crops are live size (CSS px = pt) and show the strip as regular width draws it: iPad portrait and landscape, and the unfolded iPhone Duo. iPhone and the folded Duo show no Previous or Next buttons; they page by swiping and by the page dots, so nothing changes there. Tap the chevrons to page, or use the buttons below to switch text size.

Cause

  • Measured. ChromeJourneyTests.testChromePassesAccessibilityAudit on iPad landscape failed twice with contrast 1.9 on “Previous 24-hour weather forecast”. Pixels from the same run: the glyph peaks at #4C4C4A on a circle of #1F1E1E, 1.91:1. The bar for a control is 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).
  • Why it got fainter. WeatherPagedCarousel.controlButton sets .opacity(0.4) and uses .buttonStyle(.plain). The plain style fades a disabled label a second time, so the glyph ends up at about 20% white, not 40%. Modelling both fades predicts a glyph of #4A4949 and 1.94:1, which matches the measurement. On its own, the 40% would come to about 3.9:1 on this card. The repo has seen this before: .plain faded a locked tile to 2.5:1 (patterns/entity-tile.md). That's why tiles, module rows and the Filter sheet never fade disabled text.
  • Why now. The chevron was always this faint. The audit started catching it once the one-row chip rail moved the card to where the audit samples it (branch fix/duo-tabbar-chip-rail). On main it fails the same rule; the audit just doesn't land on it.
  • The atlas's weather card mockup fades the button once, by 40%, so it shows a brighter glyph than the app draws.

Options

A · One fade, 55% (recommended)

Give the two controls a small button style that leaves disabled labels alone, like GlassTileButtonStyle and ModuleRowButtonStyle, then fade the whole control to 55%. The control looks and sits the same as before; at an end it's simply dimmer. Estimated 6.3:1 on the dark card and 4.8:1 on the lighter one; enabled is 18:1. VoiceOver still reads “dimmed”.

B · Hide at the ends

At an end, the control that can't page fades out, but its 44 pt slot stays so the hours don't move. VoiceOver skips it; the page dots and the three-finger swipe still give the position. A missing control can't fail contrast. It goes against the 2026-10-24 rule “disable instead of hiding”. That rule is about controls whose reason needs saying, which doesn't really apply to a pager, but it is a departure.

C · Outline only

At an end, the circle's fill and ring go away and the glyph stays at 50% white, so the state shows as a change of shape, not just a dimmer button. Estimated 5.1:1 on the dark card but only 3.4:1 on the lighter one, because the glyph loses the dark circle behind it. The glyph sits straight on the card, so on a bright wallpaper it has the least margin of the three, and it adds a third look to a control that otherwise has one.

Recommendation

A. It's the smallest change people would notice: the control looks and sits the same and only gets a little brighter at the ends. It fixes the actual cause (the double fade), not just this one measurement, and it's the rule tiles, rows and the Filter sheet already follow.

What it changes: WeatherPagedCarousel.swift (a private button style; .opacity(0.55) when disabled). Tests: a unit test pinning the disabled opacity, and the iPad Chrome audit rerun on iPad and the unfolded Duo, plus the weather header journeys on all three devices. Docs: patterns/weather.md, and the weather card mockup changed to fade once, by 55%.

Open question

Eight other controls fade themselves when disabled (.opacity(0.4–0.5): the bed side sheet, vacuum area editor, media remote, tinted capsule buttons and others). Any of them that also uses .plain may be faded twice in the same way. Should the fix stay on the weather card, or should I audit those eight in a follow-up?
