Proposal · 2026-10-27 proposed

Profile photos

Alex and Sam now have a photo on their Home Assistant person. The app fetches it, keeps it on the device, and draws it in place of their initials on the Profile button, the Account sheet and the People list. It fades in or out when Home Assistant's picture changes, and every cached photo is deleted on sign-out.

Owner brief 2026-10-27. Mockup-first per propose-first. The stand-in portraits below are illustrations, not real photos.

1. Goal, and where a photo goes

Today every person is initials on a filled disc. Three surfaces draw one, at three sizes (measured from source):

  • Profile button: 44 pt disc on iOS 26+ (32 pt inside a 44 pt material circle before), ProfileButton.label. Fixed size, caps Dynamic Type.
  • Account header: 84 pt, @ScaledMetric relative to .largeTitle, AccountHeader.
  • People at Home rows: 36 pt, @ScaledMetric relative to .body, PersonRow, in the person's chore colour (Alex blue, Sam magenta, guests grey).

Source. Home Assistant's person.* entity carries entity_picture (/api/image/serve/<id>/512x512) and user_id. The id inside the path changes when someone uploads a new photo, so the path is the change signal. The signed-in user's person is the one whose user_id matches auth/current_user (the rule PresenceLinker already uses). The app has no WebView and one state path, so nothing new is read: EntityStore already holds the attribute.

Not changed: the Duo rail group keeps the person symbol (a custom view stays 68 pt off the rail's column, existing departure). The welcome-back sign-in monogram keeps initials (signed out, photos are deleted).

4. Fade timing

A photo arriving or changing is a fill change of an element that stays put, which the motion doc routes to MotionTokens.state (0.6 s ease-in-out, 0.2 s under Reduce Motion), the timing the owner approved for chip, tile and fill changes. The alternative is the shorter crossFade (0.35 s), the "moment" timing. Compare them:

AD AD
0.6 s state (recommended)0.35 s crossFade

7. Recommendation

Option A with the 0.6 s state fade. It is the simplest thing that satisfies the brief, uses only existing tokens, keeps the 44 pt bar target, and treats a photo like every other avatar on iOS. The chore colours stay on the initials fallback, where they already live.

What it asks of the app.

  • HassCore PersonAvatarStore: file-per-person under Application Support (excluded from backup), a small JSON sidecar with the picture path, ETag and Last-Modified; a pure plan(cached:server:) that returns keep, download, delete or replace; removeAll(). Bearer token attached only when the URL is on the signed-in server's host.
  • HassDesign AvatarView (one canonical component, new pattern record avatar): initials under, photo layers over, two-layer cross-fade, hairline, MotionTokens.state via motionSafeAnimation.
  • HassUI PersonAvatars (observable, per-person boxes) fed from EntityStore once at launch/connect and on entity_picture change; ProfileButton, AccountHeader, PersonRow adopt AvatarView; AppSession.signOut() calls removeAll().
  • Tests: unit (plan matrix, store round-trip, sign-out wipe, host-scoped auth, change detection), UX and accessibility on iPhone, Duo and iPad (label unchanged, 44 pt target, AX5, photo is decorative, VoiceOver still reads the name).
  • VoiceOver is unchanged: the avatar stays hidden, the name is read from the button and rows.

8. Open questions

  • Expired session. sessionExpired() calls signOut(), so a refused token also deletes photos and the welcome-back screen shows initials. I read "sign-out" as including this; say if the welcome-back should keep the photo.
  • Other residents. The People list shows photos for every person.* with a picture, guests included, not only the two residents.
